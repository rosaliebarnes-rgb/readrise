# Comprehension questions & the teacher note

## Two layers of vocabulary — never collapsed

- **Layer 1 — phonics-target words:** decodable words at the student's stage. These are
  what the reading practices. Fine to feature; mark a few as practice words.
- **Layer 2 — academic vocabulary:** the conceptual words (community, culture, generation,
  hydraulic). These are **protected content** — never strip them to satisfy a phonics
  constraint. But if a Layer-2 word is too advanced to decode, keep it **out of the text**
  and hand it to the teacher to pre-teach aloud (FRONT-LOAD). The student learns it from
  the teacher, not by decoding it cold.

Stripping "community" or "culture" out of a text to hit a phonics target is a violation —
the content layer is protected. Show the idea in decodable words instead, and front-load
the academic term.

## Question design

Questions must be **decodable at the same level as the text.** A student who cannot read
the question cannot answer it. But readable is not the same as trivial:

- **No yes/no questions. Nothing answerable by guessing.** Every question must require the
  student to go back into the text and find something.
- **Concrete questions are the graded core** — pattern/word recall, evidence location,
  vocabulary matching, literal comprehension. Grade these (an 80%-correct bar on concrete
  questions is the working standard).
- **Abstract questions are bonus** — inference, connection, big idea, "what would you do."
  Include at most one or two, never as the only question, and judge them on genuine
  attempt, not correctness.
- Multiple choice is fine for recall and matching (with plausible distractors), never for
  inference or connection.

## Frames by focus

Match the teacher's stated focus. Common frames:

- **Citing evidence** — open the section with ONE directions line ("You can copy the exact
  words from the text, or write the answer in your own words") stated once, not repeated.
  Then vary each question: "Find the sentence that shows…", "Which line tells you…", "Copy
  the words that prove…", "Where does the text say…", "What line makes you think…". Each
  points to a specific place in the text.
- **Main idea / central idea / summary** — ask the reader to name the central idea,
  distinguish it from supporting details, and write a one-sentence summary.
- **Vocabulary in context** — "The text uses the word [word] in this line: [quote]. Based
  on how it is used here, what do you think it means?"
- **Analyze / connect** — trace how specific people, events, or ideas in the text connect
  to each other.
- **Argument / claim** — identify the central claim, weigh the evidence, decide if it
  convinces. (Bonus-weighted.)
- **No focus given** — 2–3 literal comprehension questions answerable directly from the
  text, plus optionally one bonus inference question.

## The teacher note

A short note **for the teacher only** — never part of what the student reads. Label it
clearly (e.g. "--- For the teacher (not for the student) ---"). Include these lines:

- **FORM** — the form used (prose, free verse, letter, dialogue) and, in one sentence, why
  — especially if you shifted away from prose to protect the level.
- **FRONT-LOAD** — the Layer-2 academic words you deliberately kept OUT of the text for the
  teacher to pre-teach aloud before reading (e.g. hydraulic, generation). If none, "none."
- **WATCH** — one sentence on what to watch for as this specific student reads (a pattern
  that may trip them, a spot to check comprehension).
- **VERIFY** — every real person, organization, date, or event named, listed so the teacher
  can fact-check before use. **Named organizations paired with specific years are the most
  error-prone** — the model will confidently mis-pair and not notice, so list those first.
  If the text asserts no specific facts, "none."
- **PRECISION** — only when no decoding stage was given: state that decodability is
  estimated from the reading level, not verified against phonics patterns, and recommend
  the free UFLI placement test (https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations/) for an exact
  stage.

The VERIFY line is not optional hedging — it is the honesty mechanism. This skill will
occasionally get a date or an affiliation wrong, and the teacher is the fact-check. Make it
easy for them to do that check by listing exactly what to confirm.
