# Decoding stages

For an **Independent** text, the student must decode it cold — using only the spelling
patterns they have been taught so far. A student's **decoding stage** is simply where they
are in that progression.

This isn't specific to any one program. Every structured-phonics curriculum — UFLI,
Fundations, SIPPS, CKLA, and the rest — teaches essentially the same code in nearly the same
order: short vowels → blends & digraphs → silent-e → vowel teams → r-controlled →
suffixes/prefixes → multisyllabic. The stages below are **labeled with UFLI's numbering**
because UFLI Foundations publishes its scope and sequence openly and for free — it's a
transparent, common map, grounded in the science of reading. A teacher who uses a different
program maps their student to the nearest stage here; a teacher who only has a reading level
uses that (decodability is then estimated, not exact — see below).

The stages are **cumulative** — a student at a later stage can read everything from the
earlier stages too.

**How to use this:** map the student's level to a stage below, then treat the stage's
DECODABLE list as your ceiling and its NOT-YET list as off-limits for *content* words.
Check every content word against the scope before committing it. Proper nouns (real names
and places) are exempt — use them freely; they carry the culture and cost nothing.

If the teacher gave a grade or Lexile rather than a stage, decodability is **estimated**,
not verified — pick the closest stage, do your best, and flag in the teacher note that an
exact stage (via the free UFLI placement test) would make it precise.

## The stages

### 1. Short vowels — UFLI L1–L41
Examples: cat, red, sit, hot, bug.
- **Decodable:** closed syllables with short vowels a/e/i/o/u; single consonants; CVC
  words; the endings -s and -ing on those words.
- **Not yet:** consonant blends (st, cl, tr, spr, nd, mp…), consonant digraphs
  (sh, ch, th, ck, wh, ng), silent-e long vowels (make, ride), vowel teams (ai, ee, oa),
  r-controlled vowels (ar, er, or), and ANY two-syllable word.
- *This stage is genuinely hard, and that's the point. Blend-heavy topics (band, drum,
  step) fight it — reach for decodable substitutes or shift to a subject that fits.*

### 2. Blends & digraphs — UFLI L42–L53
Examples: stop, ship, chin.
- **Decodable:** everything above, plus consonant blends (st, cl, tr, spr, nd, mp…) and
  consonant digraphs (sh, ch, th, ck, wh, ng).
- **Not yet:** silent-e / VCe long vowels (make, ride, home), vowel teams (ai, ee, oa),
  r-controlled vowels (ar, er, or), and multisyllabic words.

### 3. Silent-e / VCe — UFLI L54–L62
Examples: make, ride, home.
- **Decodable:** everything above, plus silent-e long vowels (VCe): make, ride, home,
  cute, these.
- **Not yet:** vowel teams (ai, ay, ee, ea, oa, ow), diphthongs (ou, oi), r-controlled
  vowels (ar, er, ir, or, ur).

### 4. Vowel teams — UFLI L63–L78
Examples: rain, boat, see.
- **Decodable:** everything above, plus vowel teams (ai, ay, ee, ea, oa, ow, oo) and
  diphthongs (ou, ow, oi, oy).
- **Not yet:** r-controlled vowels (ar, er, ir, or, ur) and multisyllabic Greek/Latin
  words.

### 5. R-controlled vowels — UFLI L79–L98
Examples: car, bird, corn.
- **Decodable:** everything above, plus r-controlled vowels (ar, er, ir, or, ur).
- **Not yet:** multisyllabic words with prefixes/suffixes and Greek/Latin roots.

### 6. Suffixes & prefixes — UFLI L99–L118
Examples: jumping, unlock.
- **Decodable:** everything above, plus common prefixes and suffixes on known roots
  (un-, re-, pre-, -ed, -ing, -ly, -ful, -less).
- **Not yet:** multisyllabic Greek and Latin root words (photo, struct, port…).

### 7. Multisyllabic / roots — UFLI L119–L128
Examples: Greek & Latin roots.
- **Decodable:** everything above, plus multisyllabic words with Greek and Latin roots.
- **Not yet:** (top of the sequence — all taught patterns are available.)

## Sight words

Common high-frequency sight words (the, a, is, was, of, to, they, said, you, when, one,
are…) are read by sight, not sounded out — they are always fine, even if they contain a
blend or digraph. This scope governs **content** words, not sight words.

## The one-hard-word escape hatch

If a core subject word simply has no decodable substitute at this stage, you may use it
**once**, then refer to "it" afterward, and list it in the teacher note under FRONT-LOAD
so the teacher pre-teaches it aloud. Everything else stays fully decodable.
